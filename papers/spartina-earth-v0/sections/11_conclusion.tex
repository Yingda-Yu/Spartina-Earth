\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}

We presented Spartina Earth, a provenance-aware multisensor framework
for long-term monitoring of the invasive cordgrass \emph{Spartina
alterniflora} across changing satellite generations. In a three-bay,
1985--2026 Zhejiang setting, an 18{,}435-scene metadata census, a
fixed observation hierarchy, and three executed correction audits
establish what a trustworthy cross-generation observation must
contain: a stable cell identity distinct from bay-scoped statistics;
an acquisition group gated only by scenes that actually observe the
cell; SAR eligibility from measured per-scene geometry; and tiered,
rights-bearing labels in which unlabeled is never negative. A small,
frozen baseline ladder demonstrates a functioning, leak-aware
engineering pipeline while making the absence of GOLD validation and
the small test support impossible to overlook. A deliberately small real-pixel multimodal pilot now verifies that
chain on eight stratified cells, including the first eligible
Landsat~8/9 and Sentinel-1 products and a fourth geometry lesson:
nominal acquisition frames of any sensor are prefilters. The target-independent national coastal frame and its metadata-only
census (184{,}051 six-sensor scenes across 3{,}319 provisional 10\,km
cells, with no pixel exports) are now built; the next steps are the
authorized deterministic first-pixel panel, label access closure,
and---only once the substrate is closed---a sensor-agnostic temporal
representation model evaluated on group-disjoint cross-region and
cross-era tracks.
