\section{Discussion}\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{Why geometry and provenance precede modeling}
The three R1 corrections share one lesson. A silently swallowed
geometry exception produced a biologically implausible zero; a quality
gate applied to the wrong spatial unit rejected every autumn event in a
whole sensor generation; and an orbit-level footprint surrogate
misclassified eligibility in both directions at large rates. None of
these failures was visible from model metrics, and none would have been
fixed by a better classifier. Treating the observation contract---what
geometry, which scenes, which quality metadata, under what rights---as
the primary object of study is therefore a prerequisite to, not a
bookkeeping consequence of, cross-generation modeling.

\subsection{Sensor-specific quality without sensor-specific science}
The framework keeps sensor-specific eligibility (actual SAR geometry,
contributing optical scenes, native resolutions) while presenting one
scientific question per cell--event. This preserves information that
premature harmonization removes: 30\,m history stays a 30\,m product,
SAR stays dB, and a multi-tile datatake is one event with its member
tiles named. Fusion and harmonization remain modeling decisions made
after provenance, which also keeps them falsifiable.

\subsection{Labels as contested evidence}
The SILVER/WEAK arbitration (Jaccard 0.49 over full overlap; 0.95 on
evaluable core pixels) and the invalid-geometry false zero both argue
against collapsing label sources into one binary target. Explicit
tiers, diagnostic disagreement layers, and separate rights records
allow a model to be trained, described, and audited against specific
evidence rather than an averaged label. Until GOLD evidence exists,
accuracy language is restricted to SILVER reproduction and engineering
behavior.

\subsection{Toward sensor-agnostic representation learning}
The temporal representation and foundation-model literature
\citep{russwurm2020selfattn,garnot2020pixelset,cong2022satmae,fuller2023croma,jakubik2024prithvi,xiong2024dofa}
provides candidate encoders for the flagship SpartinaFM question:
whether a representation remains ecologically stable across sensor
generations, regions, phenology, and management regimes, including
missing modalities at inference. Our position is that this question is
answerable only after the observation substrate is correct; cross-era
and cross-region benchmark tracks on group-disjoint cells
\citep{tuia2016da,ploton2020spatial} are the evaluation design, and no
representation result is claimed in this version.
